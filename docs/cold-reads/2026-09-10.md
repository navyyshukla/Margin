# Cold read #4, 2026-09-10

Owed by the store: `#store` is a new format line and `\@nnnn[Nt]` a new cell
encoding. Method and running score: [`docs/cold-reads.md`](../cold-reads.md).

One payload — `github_issues` with `--store`, the document the store changes
most: 3,298 tokens carrying 32 handles that stand for 19,279 tokens of content.

**This read asked something the first three did not.** Every previous read could
answer everything from the document. This one was given a document whose values
are deliberately elsewhere, and **no way to fetch them**, to answer the question
that decides whether the whole store stage is worth building:

> Does a reader who cannot fetch *say so*, or does it answer from the columns
> around the handle?

## Result: 12 of 12 correct, confidence 8/10

Every factual answer verified against the original payload. The two counting
answers were exactly right, list and all — the first read in four where counting
did not produce at least a near-miss.

## The question it was run to answer: passed

Asked to summarise an issue whose body is a handle, the reader said **"not
answerable from this document"** and named the handle. Asked to describe a body
in detail, it said **"I have zero information about its content."** It did not
infer, did not hedge, and did not answer from the title as though that were the
body.

That is the failure that would have made this stage worse than useless, and it
did not happen.

## The lure paid for itself in the same read

`\@0001[276t]` costs 0.5 percentage points against a bare `\@0001`
(`docs/thresholds.md`). Two of the twelve questions are answerable **only**
because of it, and the reader got both right without retrieving anything:

- **"Which issue has the longest body?"** — 37510, read off `[2313t]` against
  1916 and 1312. Correct. It also caught its own caveat unprompted: token cost is
  a proxy for length, not a measurement of it.
- **"What would it cost to read everything absent?"** — 19,279 tokens, summed by
  hand from 32 lures. Exactly right.

Without the lure both are "not answerable", and a reader deciding what to fetch
would have had nothing to decide on. It also said it would **fetch selectively
rather than all 19,279 at once** — the behaviour the whole design depends on.

## Self-description held

Unprompted, from the legend and the `#store` line alone, the reader produced:

> `fetch(document="22c8424b6010d312e67b69d6", ids=[...])`

It found the tool name, the document id and the id format without being told the
tool exists. Naming the tool in the legend cost a handful of tokens and removed
the last piece of out-of-band knowledge the document needed.

## The defect it found, and it is not the store's

> *"The single thing most likely to make a reader wrong: columns whose cells are
> **references rather than values**, sitting in plain sight next to columns whose
> cells are values."*

Concretely: `labels:dict` renders as `1`, which means *key 1 in the `#dict`
line*, and it sits immediately beside `comments` and `reactions.+1`, which are
genuinely small integers. A reader skimming reports "issue 37534 has 1 label".

**The handle is not the problem here — `#dict` is.** `\@0004[345t]` is visually
unmistakable; a bare `1` is not. The store read surfaced a pre-existing weakness
in an encoding two earlier reads passed over.

**Not fixed, deliberately**, on the standard cold read #1 set for `\M`: no answer
in four reads has been wrong because of it, and this format pays for legibility
when a read demonstrates the need, not on suspicion. **Revisit when a read
actually miscounts a `dict` cell as a value** — at that point it stops being a
confidence question. The fix to reach for is marking dict cells the way handles
are marked.

Three smaller things, recorded and not acted on:

- **PR-vs-issue is nowhere stated**, and had to be reverse-engineered from which
  optional columns are populated. That is a property of GitHub's payload, not of
  this format — but `pull_request.merged_at:null?`, a column whose only possible
  value is null, is a strange thing for a reader to meet.
- **`id` and `number` are both plausibly "the issue number"**; the document does
  not say which is human-facing.
- **`#const` applies silently.** A reader who skipped line 3 would report every
  constant field as missing.

## Counting, a fourth time — and it held

All four reads have leaned on counting, and this one leaned hardest: Q4, Q5 and
Q10 required counting commas across 30 lines and summing 32 numbers. It got all
three right and still flagged them as its own weak point: *"no way to verify."*

The two format changes bought by earlier reads are why. `HEADER_REPEAT_EVERY`
keeps the header near the row being read, and the keyed `#dict` line removed
index-counting. Confidence has now risen at every read — 6, 7, 8, 8 — while the
same weakness keeps being the thing the reader is least sure of.
